⚠ Data quality note — two different totals from the same company.

Reliance's BRSR filing (regulatory, Deloitte-assured) reports Scope 1+2 = 37.93 Mt CO₂e for FY 2024-25 (manufacturing operations boundary). The Annual Report natural capital page reports Scope 1+2 = 45.76 Mt CO₂e for O2C+E&P alone for the same year. The gap reflects different reporting boundaries — the BRSR covers Indian manufacturing sites; the Annual Report includes a broader consolidated scope including offshore E&P. All charts below use the BRSR figures (regulatory, assured). Annual Report segment data is shown separately and labelled accordingly.

Scope 3 Emissions

Scope 3 — Value Chain Emissions NOT DISCLOSED

What "Scope 3" means and why it matters for a company like Reliance.
Reliance does not disclose Scope 3 emissions. Both the BRSR 2024-25 and BRSR 2022-23 show an explicit dash (—) in the Scope 3 section. No figure has been published.
Why Scope 3 matters enormously for Reliance specifically:
  • Scope 3 for an oil & gas / petrochemicals company includes the emissions that occur when customers burn the fuels and use the chemicals Reliance produces. For a company of Reliance's scale, this figure would likely be 10–20× larger than its Scope 1+2 combined — potentially several hundred million tonnes of CO₂e per year. UNVERIFIED — no third-party estimate found.
  • Context: For comparison, BP's Scope 3 was ~360 Mt CO₂e in 2023 vs Scope 1+2 of ~34 Mt. Reliance's refining capacity and petrochemical output suggest a similar or larger ratio.
  • SEBI's BRSR framework classifies Scope 3 as a "Leadership Indicator" — meaning it is encouraged but not yet mandatory for Indian companies. This is why Reliance can legally leave it blank.
  • What to watch: SEBI is progressively tightening BRSR requirements. Scope 3 disclosure is expected to become mandatory for the top 250 listed companies by 2026-27.
Climate Targets & Commitments

Announced Targets

Sources: BRSR 2024-25, Annual Report 2024-25 net carbon zero page, ril.com/sustainability
  • 2029-30
    Reliance Jio (RJIL): 100% renewable electricity for all telecom operations. SBTi-validated target aligned with a 1.5°C global warming pathway.
    Source: RIL Annual Report 2024-25 — Natural Capital page
  • 2030
    Group-level: Enable 100 GW of renewable energy capacity in India. Includes the Dhirubhai Ambani Green Energy Giga Complex (5,000 acres, Jamnagar) — solar, batteries, green hydrogen.
    Source: RIL Annual Report 2024-25 — Net Carbon Zero strategy page
  • 2035
    Group-wide: Net Carbon Zero. Strategy focuses on (1) energy efficiency across O2C operations, (2) renewable energy scale-up, (3) green hydrogen production, (4) carbon capture. No specific interim milestones disclosed for O2C or Retail segments.
    Source: BRSR 2024-25, p.34 & p.37 · BRSR 2022-23, p.34
What is missing from Reliance's target disclosures:
  • No interim targets between now and 2035 for the O2C (Oil to Chemicals) segment, which produces ~96% of Scope 1 emissions.
  • No specific absolute reduction target (e.g., "reduce Scope 1 by 30% by 2030"). The Net Zero 2035 target is an endpoint, not a trajectory.
  • No Scope 3 reduction target.
  • No carbon credit or offset strategy disclosed.
  • The 100 GW renewable energy target is for enabling India's energy transition broadly — it is not the same as Reliance's own operational decarbonisation.
