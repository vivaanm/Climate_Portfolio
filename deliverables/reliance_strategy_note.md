# Reliance Industries — Decarbonisation Strategy Note
**Prepared:** 2026-07-30 | **Analyst:** Climate Portfolio Project
**Sources:** RIL BRSR 2024-25 (Deloitte-assured) · RIL Annual Report 2024-25 (Natural Capital & Net Carbon Zero pages)
**Scope:** Scope 1 and 2 reduction initiatives only. Scope 3 not assessed — no data disclosed by Reliance.

---

## Purpose

This note ranks Reliance's disclosed carbon reduction initiatives by three criteria: (1) whether they have already started or are only announced; (2) estimated cost and feasibility as disclosed in official documents; and (3) a realistic assessment of timeline credibility given the data. Every figure is cited to an exact page. Anything not found in the source documents is marked **UNVERIFIED**.

**The gap in plain English:** Reliance has reduced Scope 1+2 by approximately 0.76 Mt over four years (from 38.09 Mt to 37.93 Mt — a rate of roughly 0.19 Mt/yr). To reach Net Zero by 2035 from a current base of 37.93 Mt, it would need to eliminate or offset the equivalent of roughly 4.2 Mt per year for each of the next nine years — more than 20× the current pace of reduction. No pathway describing how this acceleration will be achieved is disclosed in either document.

## Ranking of Disclosed Initiatives

Initiatives are ranked from most to least credible based on: operational status, availability of quantified evidence, and alignment between stated ambition and demonstrated progress.

### TIER 1 — Operational, verified, producing measurable results

---

#### 1. Operational Energy Conservation: Gas Turbine Upgrades, DHDS Heat Integration, Vent Gas Recovery

**What it is:** Three named engineering modifications at Reliance's O2C manufacturing sites — upgrading gas turbines with "advanced gas path components" to burn less fuel for the same output; integrating process heat in the Diesel Hydro Desulfurization (DHDS) plant to reduce heating demand; and recovering vent gas from a Deisopentanizer column that would otherwise be flared or wasted.

**What DHDS means in plain English:** DHDS is an industrial process that removes sulphur from diesel fuel. It requires high-temperature heating. "Process heat integration" means capturing waste heat from one part of the plant and using it to supply another, rather than burning additional fuel. Standard engineering practice in advanced refineries.

**Status:** Implemented in FY2024-25. Described as completed initiatives. ✅ **STARTED AND OPERATIONAL**

**Evidence of impact:** The BRSR states these contributed to GHG reduction measures in FY2024-25 alongside fuel switching (p.37). No standalone CO₂ saving figure is disclosed for individual schemes. **UNVERIFIED — quantum of saving not stated.**

**Cost:** Not disclosed. Energy efficiency projects of this type typically have payback periods of 2–5 years and are funded from routine capital expenditure budgets. **UNVERIFIED — no figure in source documents.**

**Feasibility:** High. These are proven technologies applied in an already-operating refinery. No new infrastructure required.

**Timeline credibility:** These are operational now. Contribution is real but incremental — unlikely to drive large absolute reductions independently.

**Citation:** BRSR 2024-25, p.37 (Q.8 — GHG reduction projects section)

---

#### 2. Biomass Co-firing at Four Manufacturing Sites

**What it is:** Reliance's sites at Hazira, Dahej, Hoshiarpur, and Barabanki use biomass (agricultural residue — crop stalks, husks) blended with coal as fuel in boilers and furnaces. Burning biomass instead of coal reduces net CO₂ emissions because the carbon in biomass was recently absorbed from the atmosphere by crops — it is "carbon-circular" in accounting terms, though not carbon-free.

**Status:** Ongoing in FY2024-25. Described as continued usage. ✅ **STARTED AND OPERATIONAL**

**Evidence:** Named explicitly in BRSR 2024-25 p.37 as a current GHG reduction measure. No percentage substitution rate or CO₂ saving is disclosed. **UNVERIFIED — scale not quantified.**

**Cost:** Not disclosed. Biomass is typically cheaper than coal in India, making this both an emissions and cost reduction measure. **UNVERIFIED — no figure in source documents.**

**Feasibility:** High for existing sites. Limited by biomass supply chain reliability and moisture content (wet biomass reduces combustion efficiency). Not scalable to the full refinery — Jamnagar's scale dwarfs the four named sites.

**Timeline credibility:** Operational now. Contributes continuously. Scale of contribution unknown.

**Citation:** BRSR 2024-25, p.37 (Q.8)

#### 3. Fuel Switching: Carbon-Rich to Carbon-Lean Fuel at Silvassa

**What it is:** The Silvassa manufacturing division (textiles and chemicals, Gujarat) switched from a higher-carbon fuel source to a lower-carbon alternative — likely from coal to natural gas, or from heavy fuel oil to pipeline gas. Natural gas produces roughly 40–50% less CO₂ per unit of energy than coal.

**Status:** Implemented in FY2024-25. ✅ **STARTED AND OPERATIONAL**

**Evidence:** Named in BRSR 2024-25 p.37. No CO₂ saving figure disclosed. **UNVERIFIED — quantum not stated.**

**Cost:** Not disclosed. Fuel switching typically requires capital for new burners/pipelines and has ongoing fuel cost implications depending on relative gas vs. coal prices. **UNVERIFIED.**

**Feasibility:** High for Silvassa (a smaller site). Not directly transferable to Jamnagar refinery — which runs on complex refinery fuel gas, not a simple fuel type that can be easily substituted.

**Timeline credibility:** Operational. Silvassa is not a major emissions source; contribution to group-level reduction is likely modest.

**Citation:** BRSR 2024-25, p.37 (Q.8)

#### 4. PAT Scheme Compliance (Performance, Achieve and Trade)

**What it is:** SEBI's PAT scheme requires India's largest industrial energy consumers (called "Designated Consumers") to meet specific energy efficiency targets set by the Bureau of Energy Efficiency (BEE). Reliance's sites — the Jamnagar refinery (DTA and SEZ), Dahej Chlor-Alkali, Hazira/Dahej/Vadodara/Nagothane petrochemical crackers, and Naroda textile — are all DCs.

**Why it matters:** PAT is a regulatory mandate, not a voluntary initiative. Compliance is legally required. Achieving PAT targets generates Energy Savings Certificates (ESCerts) which can be sold to other companies that fail to meet their targets.

**Status:** Ongoing across multiple PAT cycles. The Jamnagar DTA refinery was issued 26,036 ESCerts in PAT Cycle 1. Dahej Chlor-Alkali received 11,949 ESCerts. Current PAT Cycle 7 (2022-2025) is in progress for all sites. ✅ **STARTED AND OPERATIONAL**

**Evidence of impact:** ESCert issuance confirmed in BRSR 2024-25 p.35. Note: BEE verification of audited ESCerts for the refinery's second PAT cycle (2020-2023) and the petrochemical PAT cycle (2018-2022) is still awaited — meaning the most recent verified data is incomplete.

**Cost:** Not disclosed. Energy efficiency investment required to meet targets; partially offset by ESCert revenue. **UNVERIFIED.**

**Feasibility:** High — Reliance has a track record across multiple cycles.

**Timeline credibility:** Established regulatory programme. Delivers incremental improvement reliably.

**Citation:** BRSR 2024-25, p.35 (Q.2 — PAT scheme)

#### 5. Circular Plastics from Pyrolysis Oil (CircuRelene® / CircuRepol®)

**What it is:** Reliance processes waste plastic into "pyrolysis oil" — a liquid fuel produced by heating plastic without oxygen — and co-processes it in the Jamnagar refinery to produce circular-economy polymers (CircuRelene® polyethylene and CircuRepol® polypropylene). This reduces the need for virgin fossil feedstock and diverts plastic waste from landfill.

**Status:** Operational and certified. Reliance holds ISCC Plus certification (an international sustainability standard for circular materials) for the second consecutive year as of August 2024. ✅ **STARTED AND OPERATIONAL**

**Evidence:** BRSR 2024-25 p.39 (Q.4 — specific initiatives table). ISCC Plus certification confirmed.

**Cost:** Not disclosed. **UNVERIFIED.**

**Feasibility:** High at current scale. This is a feedstock substitution initiative, not a direct GHG reduction — its effect on Scope 1 emissions is indirect (lower energy intensity per tonne of virgin polymer avoided). Does not materially reduce refinery combustion emissions.

**Timeline credibility:** Already delivered. Contribution to Net Zero trajectory is real but not primary — this is a circular economy initiative more than a carbon elimination one.

**Citation:** BRSR 2024-25, p.39 (specific initiatives table, Q.4)

### TIER 2 — Announced, some early-stage activity, timelines uncertain

---

#### 6. Reliance Jio: 100% Renewable Electricity by FY2029-30

**What it is:** Jio (Reliance's telecom business) has committed to sourcing 100% of its electricity from renewable sources by FY2029-30. This has been validated by the Science Based Targets initiative (SBTi) as consistent with a 1.5°C global warming pathway.

**Status:** Announced with SBTi validation. ⚠ **ANNOUNCED — partial implementation underway** (renewable electricity already partially sourced; full 100% target is forward-looking)

**Evidence:** Annual Report 2024-25 (Natural Capital page). Jio Scope 2 FY2024-25 = 1.05 Mt CO₂e (Annual Report, Natural Capital page). Full renewable switch would eliminate most of this.

**Cost:** Not disclosed in either document. Renewable PPAs (Power Purchase Agreements) for a telecom company of Jio's scale are typically multi-billion rupee commitments. **UNVERIFIED.**

**Feasibility:** High for a telecom company. Renewable electricity procurement via PPAs or open-access agreements is well-established in India. Jio's energy consumption — while large in absolute terms — is primarily electricity (unlike the O2C business which uses process heat), making it amenable to electrification via renewables.

**Timeline credibility:** 2029-30 is feasible given the SBTi validation and current pace of renewable development in India. This is the most credible specific target in Reliance's disclosed portfolio.

**Estimated impact:** Elimination of ~1 Mt CO₂e from Jio Scope 2 — less than 3% of total Scope 1+2. Meaningful for Jio; small relative to group total.

**Citation:** Annual Report 2024-25, Natural Capital page

#### 7. Battery Giga Factory (30 GWh capacity)

**What it is:** A large-scale battery manufacturing facility as part of the Dhirubhai Ambani Green Energy Giga Complex at Jamnagar. Intended to produce batteries for energy storage (enabling intermittent solar/wind power to be stored and used when needed) and potentially for electric vehicles.

**Status:** Equipment delivery expected in 2025; progressive commissioning 2025-2026 per Annual Report. ⚠ **ANNOUNCED — pre-commissioning**

**Evidence:** Annual Report 2024-25 (Net Carbon Zero strategy page). No construction completion confirmed in BRSR 2024-25.

**Cost:** Not disclosed in either document. **UNVERIFIED.** Industry comparable: a 30 GWh battery factory represents a capital investment of approximately USD 3–6 billion at current industry benchmarks. **This figure is UNVERIFIED — not in source documents.**

**Feasibility:** Medium. Battery manufacturing at giga-scale is technically complex and requires secured supply chains for lithium, cobalt, and other critical minerals. Reliance has not disclosed its supply chain arrangements for these materials. Commissioning of the first GWh of capacity is achievable by 2026; full 30 GWh timeline is uncertain.

**Timeline credibility:** Equipment delivery stated for 2025. Progressive commissioning is a cautious framing — this is not a committed delivery date. **UNVERIFIED — no completion date stated.**

**Impact on Reliance's own Scope 1:** Indirect only. The factory, once operational, would produce batteries used elsewhere. It does not reduce emissions from the refinery.

**Citation:** Annual Report 2024-25 (Net Carbon Zero strategy page)

#### 8. 100 GW of Renewable Energy by 2030 (Group-Level Enabling Target)

**What it is:** Reliance has committed to "establish and enable" 100 GW of renewable energy capacity by 2030. This is a group-level target that includes solar and wind capacity built and potentially sold or licensed to third parties — not exclusively for Reliance's own operational consumption.

**Status:** ⚠ **ANNOUNCED — planning stage.** The Dhirubhai Ambani Green Energy Giga Complex (5,000 acres, Jamnagar) is the primary site. Construction details not disclosed in BRSR.

**Evidence:** Annual Report 2024-25 (Net Carbon Zero strategy page). Also mentioned at ril.com/sustainability.

**Cost:** Not disclosed in either document. **UNVERIFIED.** Industry comparable: 100 GW of solar capacity at current Indian costs (~INR 4–5 crore/MW) would represent approximately INR 4–5 lakh crore (USD 50–60 billion). **This figure is UNVERIFIED — not in source documents.**

**Feasibility:** Low-to-medium at the stated scale and timeline. 100 GW by 2030 is 4 years away. India's entire installed renewable capacity as of 2024 is approximately 200 GW — adding 100 GW through one company in 4 years would be historically unprecedented. The "enable" language (not "build") suggests Reliance may be counting capacity it facilitates through investment, joint ventures, or technology licensing, not solely what it constructs. **UNVERIFIED — definition of "enable" not clarified in source documents.**

**Timeline credibility:** Low for 100 GW by 2030 at face value. Directionally credible as a signal of intent.

**Impact on Reliance's Scope 1:** Indirect only. If Reliance uses renewable power in its own operations, it reduces Scope 2. Scope 1 (combustion at refineries) is unaffected by renewable electricity unless electrification of process heat occurs — a major unsolved engineering challenge at refinery scale. **UNVERIFIED — no electrification pathway disclosed.**

**Citation:** Annual Report 2024-25 (Net Carbon Zero strategy page); ril.com/sustainability

---

#### 9. Green Hydrogen Electrolyser (Multi-GW Facility)

**What it is:** Green hydrogen is produced by splitting water using electricity from renewable sources. It can replace natural gas or hydrogen derived from fossil fuels in refinery processes, dramatically reducing Scope 1 emissions. A multi-GW electrolyser is required to produce hydrogen at a scale meaningful for refinery operations.

**Status:** ⚠ **ANNOUNCED — pre-development.** The Annual Report describes a "multi-GW electrolyser facility" but no capacity, cost, location, or timeline is specified beyond the Jamnagar Giga Complex setting.

**Evidence:** Annual Report 2024-25 (Net Carbon Zero strategy page). No mention in BRSR 2024-25 of green hydrogen progress or investment.

**Cost:** Not disclosed. **UNVERIFIED.** Green hydrogen at scale remains one of the most capital-intensive decarbonisation technologies globally — typically USD 2–5 per kg of H₂ at current costs, requiring billions in electrolyser investment alone. **This figure is UNVERIFIED — not in source documents.**

**Feasibility:** Low-to-medium at the scale needed to decarbonise Jamnagar. Refinery hydrogen demand is enormous; current green hydrogen production globally is a tiny fraction of industrial demand. The technology exists but cost-competitiveness and scale remain major challenges.

**Timeline credibility:** No timeline disclosed. Described as aspirational. **UNVERIFIED — no commissioning date stated.**

**Potential impact if delivered:** This is the most strategically significant initiative for actual Scope 1 reduction. If Jamnagar's hydrogen supply were decarbonised, Scope 1 emissions could fall substantially. But without a disclosed timeline, cost, or capacity target, this remains a stated direction rather than a credible near-term commitment.

**Citation:** Annual Report 2024-25 (Net Carbon Zero strategy page)

## Strategic Assessment

**The honest picture:** Reliance's operational initiatives (Tier 1) are real, credible, and ongoing — but they are producing reductions of well under 1 Mt CO₂e per year against a base of 37.93 Mt. At this pace, 2035 Net Zero is not achievable through operational efficiency alone.

The transformational initiatives that could actually move the needle — green hydrogen for refinery use, full electrification of process heat, carbon capture — are either absent from the disclosures or described without timelines, costs, or committed targets.

**What would make this strategy credible:**
1. A quantified annual reduction target for O2C Scope 1 emissions between now and 2035
2. A green hydrogen commissioning date and capacity figure for Jamnagar
3. Disclosure of what role, if any, carbon credits will play in bridging the remaining gap
4. A Scope 3 assessment — because a refinery that reaches Net Zero operations but continues at full throughput has not addressed its primary climate impact

**The Jio exception:** Jio's SBTi-validated 100% renewables target by FY2029-30 is the most credible specific commitment in this portfolio. It is modest in absolute CO₂ terms but sets a useful template — third-party validated, specific, time-bound — that the O2C business lacks entirely.
