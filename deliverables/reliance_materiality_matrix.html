What is a Double Materiality Matrix?

The CSRD (Corporate Sustainability Reporting Directive) is the EU's mandatory sustainability reporting standard. It requires companies to assess each issue on two axes at once:

Financial materiality (x-axis →): Does this issue create financial risk or opportunity for Reliance? A carbon tax would raise Reliance's operating costs — that is high financial materiality for Reliance.

Impact materiality (y-axis ↑): Does Reliance's activity cause harm or benefit to people and planet on this issue? Reliance's refinery emissions affect air quality for communities near Jamnagar — that is high impact materiality for the world.

Issues in the top-right quadrant are "double material" — they matter financially and they matter for the world. These get the most scrutiny from investors and regulators. Scores are on a 1–9 scale. Where our independent analysis disagrees with Reliance's own framing, both are shown.

Independent Discrepancy Analysis — Where Our Judgment Differs from Reliance's

Climate Change DISCREPANCY

Reliance's self-framing (BRSR p.12): Frames climate change primarily as a risk to Reliance — physical risks (flooding, heat) and transitional risks (policy, energy markets). Their implicit impact score: medium (~5/9).

Our independent assessment: Reliance is also a major cause of climate change. Scope 1+2 = 37.93 Mt CO₂e (BRSR p.36). Scope 3 = not disclosed, but for an oil refinery likely 300–400 Mt/year based on global peer ratios. Impact score: 9/9.

The gap: By not disclosing Scope 3, Reliance's materiality language obscures that its core product — fossil fuel — is the mechanism by which it contributes to the climate problem it is simultaneously treating as a financial risk.

Sources: BRSR 2024-25 p.12 (materiality), p.36 (Scope 1+2); Scope 3 peer ratio from BP 2023 annual report (UNVERIFIED for Reliance)

Managing Environmental Impacts (Air Quality) DISCREPANCY

Reliance's self-framing (BRSR p.12): Framed as regulatory compliance and operational cost management. Their implicit impact score: medium (~5/9).

Our independent assessment: BRSR p.36 discloses 33,972 tonnes of NOx, 14,116 tonnes of SOx, and 48,465 tonnes of VOC in FY2024-25. NOx and SOx at this scale cause significant respiratory illness in communities near Jamnagar, Dahej, and Hazira. This is a high-impact issue for surrounding populations — not merely a compliance cost. Impact score: 8/9.

The gap: The BRSR never uses the phrase "community health impact" in the materiality section for this issue. The framing is entirely inward-facing.

Source: BRSR 2024-25 p.12 (materiality language), p.36 (NOx/SOx/VOC figures, Deloitte-assured)

Water & Effluent Management DISCREPANCY

Reliance's self-framing (BRSR p.12): Framed as operational risk — water availability may become a concern for Reliance's own operations. Their implicit impact score: medium (~6/9).

Our independent assessment: Total water withdrawal is 20.84 billion kilolitres per year (BRSR p.35 — Deloitte-assured). Of this, 9.72 billion kL comes from surface water (rivers, lakes) in a country where water stress is severe and growing. The materiality language does not discuss ecological or community impacts of this withdrawal scale. Impact score: 7/9.

The gap: Reliance is one of India's largest industrial water users. Its impact on downstream communities and ecosystems is not addressed in its own materiality assessment.

Source: BRSR 2024-25 p.12 (materiality), p.35 (water data, Deloitte-assured)

What "no discrepancy" means

For issues like Raw Material Security, Innovation & Technology, Disaster Preparedness, Talent Management, and Community Development, our independent assessment largely agrees with Reliance's own framing. These are either: issues where Reliance's impact on the world is genuinely limited (Raw Materials, Disaster Preparedness), or where the framing is balanced and reasonable (Community Development, Health & Safety).

The three discrepancies flagged above share a pattern: in each case, Reliance frames the issue as a risk to itself when the data shows it is also a significant cause of impact on the world. This is a known bias in corporate materiality assessments — companies tend to see issues through the lens of how they are affected, not how they affect others.
