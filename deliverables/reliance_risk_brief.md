# Reliance Industries — Physical Climate Risk Brief
**Prepared by:** Climate Portfolio Project
**Date:** 2026-08-02
**Sites assessed:** 10 manufacturing site clusters (source: BRSR 2024-25 p.48, Deloitte-assured)
**Hazard data:** ThinkHazard (GFDRR / World Bank); WRI Aqueduct 4.0 (country level)

---

## What this is and why it matters

A **physical climate risk register** maps where a company's physical assets sit — refineries, chemical plants, factories — against the natural hazards that could damage those assets or interrupt operations. For a lender, this tells you whether the collateral behind a loan could flood, overheat, or lose access to water. For an equity investor, it tells you where capital expenditure on climate-proofing may be unavoidable.

Reliance Industries is India's largest company by revenue. Its manufacturing operations are almost entirely concentrated along India's west coast and in the Indo-Gangetic plain — two of the regions most exposed to climate hazards in the coming decades.

## Finding 1: Eight of ten sites face HIGH overall physical risk

Seven of the ten sites sit in Gujarat or on the Maharashtra coast — directly in the Arabian Sea cyclone belt. ThinkHazard (the World Bank's public hazard tool) rates **cyclone risk as High** across all of Gujarat and the Maharashtra coast, meaning there is more than a 20% chance of damaging winds in any given 10-year period.

The same coastal cluster faces **High coastal flood risk** from storm surges. Jamnagar's refinery complex — the largest in the world at 1.24 million barrels per day capacity — sits on the Saurashtra peninsula, a semi-arid coastline with no perennial rivers. A direct cyclone landfall or a severe storm surge at Jamnagar would affect irreplaceable industrial infrastructure with no close substitute.

Barabanki in Uttar Pradesh is inland but faces **High river flood risk**: it sits on the Ganga-Ghaghra plain, where rivers regularly breach in severe monsoon years.

---

## Finding 2: Every single site faces HIGH extreme heat risk

ThinkHazard rates extreme heat as High for Gujarat, Maharashtra, Uttar Pradesh, Dadra & Nagar Haveli, and Hoshiarpur district — which together cover all ten sites. This means prolonged heat stress is expected at least once every five years at each location.

This matters operationally. Outdoor work in refinery and petrochemical environments becomes unsafe or legally prohibited above certain wet-bulb temperatures. India's heat extremes are projected to intensify by 1.5–2°C above current levels by 2050 under IPCC moderate-emissions scenarios. A climate that already pushes the limits of outdoor work in summer will push further beyond them.

---

## Finding 3: Gujarat's chronic water shortage is the slowest-burning risk

ThinkHazard rates **water scarcity as High** for Gujarat — affecting Jamnagar, Dahej, Hazira, Vadodara, and Naroda. WRI Aqueduct 4.0 ranks India as the 13th most water-stressed country in the world (source: WRI press release, 2023), with Gujarat cited among India's most stressed states.

The Jamnagar refinery requires very large volumes of freshwater for processing and cooling. Saurashtra — the peninsula where the complex sits — has no perennial rivers and depends on seasonal monsoon and groundwater recharge. In a climate that makes monsoon more variable and intensifies drought in dry years, process water security becomes a material business risk. Reliance has partially addressed this by operating Silvassa as a Zero Liquid Discharge site (BRSR p.36) — but no disclosure is made about water sourcing arrangements or contingency planning at Jamnagar.

---

## Finding 4: Reliance's own BRSR does not disclose a quantified physical risk assessment

The BRSR 2024-25 (p.13) lists "Disaster Preparedness and Management" as a material issue and states that sites are designed with a "loss prevention design philosophy." However, it does not disclose:

- Flood return periods for any site (e.g. "this site is protected to a 1-in-100-year flood event")
- Sea-level rise scenarios or coastal protection assessments
- Heat threshold analysis or workforce heat stress protocols
- Quantified financial exposure to any physical hazard

This means there is no publicly verifiable basis for assessing whether Reliance's loss prevention infrastructure is adequate for current hazards, let alone for 2035–2050 climate projections. For any lender or investor applying TCFD (Task Force on Climate-related Financial Disclosures) standards, this is a disclosure gap.

---

## What a lender or investor should do with this

1. **Request site-level physical risk assessments** from Reliance as a condition of financing — specifically asking for flood return period data and sea-level rise scenarios for all coastal Gujarat and Maharashtra sites.

2. **Apply particular scrutiny to Jamnagar.** The concentration of the world's largest refinery complex at a single coastal, semi-arid, high-cyclone location is the highest single-asset physical risk in this register.

3. **Use WRI Aqueduct directly** to query site-level water stress ratings for each location. The interactive tool (wri.org/applications/aqueduct/water-risk-atlas/) provides more granular scores than were accessible for this brief — site-level scores should be verified before use in formal credit analysis.

4. **Monitor extreme heat disclosures.** As India's heat exposure grows, lender covenants on sustainability-linked loans to industrial companies should include workforce heat protocols as a key performance indicator.

## Data notes and UNVERIFIED flags

- **All ThinkHazard ratings** are from publicly accessible state- and district-level reports, fetched directly. They represent the probability of a hazard occurring in a 10-year window, not projected future climate.
- **WRI Aqueduct** is cited at India country level only. Site-level Aqueduct water stress scores are **UNVERIFIED** — the interactive tool requires manual querying and was not accessible programmatically. Users should verify directly at wri.org/applications/aqueduct/water-risk-atlas/.
- **Jamnagar coordinates** reflect the refinery complex location (near Moti Khavdi / Sikka), not Jamnagar city.
- This brief has **not been validated by Reliance Industries**. It is an independent external assessment.
